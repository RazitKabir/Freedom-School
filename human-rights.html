Explore the Youth Plan

A Youth-Led Human Rights Vision for Kalamazoo

Young people study fairness, listen to community experiences, and organize informed action for dignity and belonging.

Through Freedom Schools and the Commonfolk Republic Civic Design Studio, scholars connect literacy, history, research, civic participation, and advocacy to the Youth-Led Master Plan. They learn that rights are lived through housing, education, mobility, health, food, technology, safety, culture, and public decision-making.

The Civic Design Studio

How human-rights scholars learn and lead

The pathway follows the Commonfolk Republic developmental arc, moving from understanding rights to sustained civic stewardship.

01

Foundations

Build shared language around dignity, rights, equity, power, responsibility, advocacy, and respectful dialogue.

02

Exploration

Study history, interview residents, examine policies, map access, and compare how systems affect different people.

03

Application

Create issue briefs, campaigns, public forums, service projects, testimony, media, and community recommendations.

04

Mastery

Facilitate dialogue, build coalitions, evaluate impact, mentor peers, and support long-term Master Plan accountability.

What Scholars Can Study

Every community system can be examined through fairness, access, voice, and accountability

Community Systems to Examine

  • Housing stability, land access, and neighborhood investment
  • Transportation, disability access, and safe mobility
  • Education, digital equity, and opportunities to learn
  • Food access, healthcare, environmental conditions, and safety
  • Representation, policing, governance, and community decision-making

Questions Scholars Can Ask

  • Who benefits, who carries the burden, and who is missing?
  • Can everyone access this system with dignity?
  • Who has decision-making power and whose voice is heard?
  • What evidence shows unequal outcomes or barriers?
  • What action can scholars take with, not only for, the community?

Projects We Can Start

From awareness to informed collective action

01

Community Rights Map

Map access to food, transit, parks, healthcare, technology, and learning spaces, then identify equity gaps.

02

Youth Listening Campaign

Interview residents and youth about belonging, barriers, safety, and hopes for Kalamazoo.

03

Issue Brief and Action Plan

Research one community concern and publish a clear brief with evidence, lived experience, and practical recommendations.

04

People's Assembly

Host a youth-facilitated forum where residents discuss a Master Plan topic and identify shared priorities.

05

Know Your Rights Media

Create posters, videos, podcasts, or social posts explaining rights and local resources in accessible language.

06

Beloved Community Campaign

Design a positive advocacy campaign that combines education, storytelling, partnership, and measurable community action.

Our Message to Kalamazoo

Young people are rights holders, advocates, bridge builders, and civic leaders.

Human Rights and Advocacy gives scholars the knowledge and tools to ask who is included, organize with integrity, and help ensure that the Youth-Led Master Plan builds dignity, justice, belonging, and shared responsibility into every community system.
