Explore the Youth Plan

A Youth-Led Arts Vision for Kalamazoo

Young people use creativity to understand community, preserve stories, and imagine a more beautiful and just future.

Through Freedom Schools and the Commonfolk Republic Civic Design Studio, scholars explore visual art, music, dance, theater, film, design, and storytelling as tools for literacy, belonging, public dialogue, and community transformation.

🎨

Visual Arts & Design

Create murals, posters, photography, sculpture, digital art, and public installations that communicate community ideas.

🎭

Theater & Performance

Use drama, spoken word, and performance to explore history, identity, conflict, and possible futures.

🎶

Music & Movement

Study rhythm, dance, sound, and cultural traditions while creating performances that connect generations.

📖

Storytelling & Media

Document oral histories, produce short films, podcasts, exhibitions, and digital stories about Kalamazoo.

🏘️

Creative Placemaking

Use art to strengthen public spaces, neighborhood identity, wayfinding, celebration, and belonging.

The Civic Design Studio

How arts scholars learn and lead

The pathway follows the Commonfolk Republic developmental arc, moving from creative foundations to community-centered artistic leadership.

01

Foundations

Build artistic skills, creative confidence, cultural awareness, collaboration, and a shared language for critique.

02

Exploration

Visit cultural spaces, interview artists and residents, study neighborhood stories, and observe how people experience place.

03

Application

Create performances, exhibitions, public art, media, and design concepts responding to real community questions.

04

Mastery

Curate community experiences, lead creative teams, mentor younger scholars, and contribute art-based recommendations to the Master Plan.

What Scholars Can Study

Culture, identity, and public life become a creative learning laboratory

Kalamazoo Stories and Spaces

  • Neighborhood histories, memories, and cultural traditions
  • Public art, parks, gathering places, and community landmarks
  • Music, theater, dance, festivals, and local creative economies
  • Representation in schools, media, museums, and public spaces
  • How design affects comfort, safety, identity, and belonging

Questions Scholars Can Ask

  • Whose stories are visible, celebrated, or missing?
  • How does this place make people feel?
  • How can art help residents listen to one another?
  • What creative work could make a space more welcoming?
  • How can artists contribute to the Beloved Community?

Projects We Can Start

From imagination to community expression

01

Community Story Mural

Collect neighborhood memories and transform them into a collaborative mural or portable public-art installation.

02

Voices of Kalamazoo

Record interviews, poems, music, and oral histories for a youth-led podcast, film, or digital archive.

03

Beloved Community Theater

Create short scenes about fairness, belonging, conflict, and community solutions, followed by public discussion.

04

Creative Placemaking Lab

Redesign a park, corridor, classroom, or gathering space using color, signage, sculpture, and performance.

05

Cultural Food Stories

Combine visual art, performance, and interviews to explore family recipes, migration, agriculture, and identity.

06

Master Plan Exhibition

Turn scholar research into an interactive exhibition with maps, models, performances, photography, and calls to action.

Our Message to Kalamazoo

Young people are artists, storytellers, culture keepers, and designers of public imagination.

Visual and Performing Arts helps scholars express what data alone cannot show. It makes community experiences visible, builds belonging, and helps the Youth-Led Master Plan become a living story of the Beloved Community.
